Why Europe

Why Bangladeshi Students Choose Europe

Eight reasons Europe delivers the lowest cost per degree
of any major study destination.

Free or Near-Free Tuition

German public universities charge no tuition at all, France charges around €3,000 and Italy assesses fees against family income — often the lowest band.

Eight Countries, One Region

Apply to several countries in the same cycle. If one visa is refused you still have live offers elsewhere, which no single-country application gives you.

Schengen Travel Included

A residence permit in any Schengen country lets you travel freely across 29 European countries — no separate visa for weekends or internships abroad.

Study Fully in English

Finland, Sweden and the Netherlands teach nearly all master’s programmes in English, and Germany, Hungary and Poland run hundreds of English-taught degrees.

Large Bangladeshi Community

Long-settled Bangladeshi communities in Italy, France and Germany, with student associations and Bangla-speaking support networks in most major cities.

Post-Study Work Everywhere

Every country here grants a job-search permit after graduation — from nine months in Hungary to two full years in Finland.

The EU Blue Card Route

Once employed, the EU Blue Card gives a fast, transferable route to long-term residence — and it moves with you between member states.

Scholarships Are Common

Finnish universities routinely waive 50–100% of tuition, Italy’s DSU awards cover accommodation and meals, and Erasmus+ runs across the whole region.

What You Need

Admission Requirements

Common checklist across the eight countries —
each has its own portal and a few extra steps.

Academic Requirements

  • HSC for undergraduate entry; some countries expect an extra year, since most EU bachelor's follow 13 years of schooling
  • Bachelor's with roughly CGPA 3.0+ for most master's programmes; Germany looks for a close subject match
  • Completed SSC, HSC and Bachelor's transcripts, translated and attested
  • Certificate attestation through the Ministry of Education and MOFA, then embassy legalisation
  • GRE/GMAT rarely required, though some German and Dutch programmes still ask for it
  • Statement of Purpose, CV and two academic references

English Proficiency

  • IELTS Academic 6.0–6.5 overall covers most English-taught programmes across all eight countries
  • The Netherlands and Finland often ask for 6.5 with no band below 6.0
  • TOEFL iBT and PTE Academic accepted almost everywhere; Duolingo at a growing number
  • Some German and Italian universities accept an MOI certificate — confirm case by case
  • A1/A2 in the local language is worth starting early: it is rarely required for admission but changes your job prospects

Financial Requirements

  • Germany: €11,904 in a blocked account (Sperrkonto) — €992 per month for twelve months
  • France, Italy, Poland: proof of roughly €600–900 per month of living costs, plus accommodation
  • Netherlands, Finland, Sweden: €900–1,500 per month, usually shown as a bank balance
  • Funds must be genuine, legally sourced and traceable — sudden large deposits are a common refusal trigger
  • Sponsor income proof, tax returns and relationship documents where a parent or relative funds you
  • Scholarship or university award letters, where applicable

Other Documents

  • Valid passport with at least six months validity beyond your intended stay
  • University admission or enrolment letter, plus proof of tuition payment where required
  • Health insurance valid across the Schengen area for the full period
  • Accommodation proof — a rental contract, dormitory letter or host declaration
  • Biometrics and, for several countries, an interview at the embassy or VFS in Dhaka
  • Country-specific step: APS certificate for Germany, Campus France interview for France, Universitaly pre-enrolment for Italy
  • Police clearance and, for some countries, a medical certificate

What You Can Do During & After Studies

What a European student residence permit allows
during your studies and after graduating.

During Studies

15–20 hrs/week

Germany allows 140 full days or 280 half days a year, the Netherlands 16 hours a week, and most others 20. Nearly everywhere lets you work full-time during official holidays.

After Graduation

12 to 24 months

Finland gives two years, Germany eighteen months, and France, Italy, Sweden, Poland and the Netherlands twelve. You apply from inside the country before your student permit expires.

Longer-Term Route

EU Blue Card

With a graduate-level job you can move onto the EU Blue Card, which carries a salary threshold of roughly €45,300 and counts towards permanent residence. Its real advantage is portability: it moves with you between member states, so the country you study in need not be the country you settle in.

FAQ

Common Questions About Studying in Europe

Quick answers to common questions
from Bangladeshi students.

Which European country should I choose?
Start from what you can fund, not from rankings. If your budget is tight, Germany, Poland, Hungary and Italy are where the numbers work — Germany charges no tuition, and Hungary runs a fully funded government scholarship for Bangladeshi students. If you want to study and work entirely in English, the Netherlands, Finland and Sweden are the strongest. If the longest possible stay-back matters most, Finland gives a full twenty-four months. We usually shortlist two or three countries rather than one.
What IELTS score do I need for Europe?
There is no visa-level English requirement in most of Europe — the score is set by each university. IELTS 6.0–6.5 overall covers the great majority of English-taught programmes. The Netherlands and Finland more often ask for 6.5 with no band below 6.0. A few German and Italian universities accept an MOI certificate from a Bangladeshi institution, but confirm that programme by programme rather than assuming it.
How much money do I need to show for a European student visa?
It differs by country. Germany is the strictest and the clearest: €11,904 deposited in a blocked account, released to you at €992 a month. France, Italy and Poland want proof of roughly €600–900 a month; the Netherlands, Finland and Sweden want €900–1,500. In every case the source matters as much as the amount — funds must be genuine, legally earned and traceable, and a balance that appears days before you apply invites refusal.
Which intake is best for Bangladeshi students applying to Europe?
The autumn intake — September or October depending on the country — is the main one everywhere. Every course runs, scholarship rounds are open, and the visa queue has not yet peaked. Start about twelve months ahead, because document attestation alone can take two to three months from Bangladesh. January and February give a smaller but genuine second cycle. The spring intake is essentially Germany only.
Can my spouse come with me to Europe?
This is one of the biggest differences between these eight countries, and it is worth checking before you choose. Germany is the most open: spouses on family reunion get full, unrestricted work rights. Several others only allow dependants once you have moved from a student permit onto a work permit. Everywhere you will need to show additional funds for each family member. We check the current position country by country rather than generalising, because these rules change often.
Does each country have its own extra step?
Yes, and this is where most delays happen. Germany needs an APS certificate verifying your Bangladeshi qualifications, plus a blocked account holding €11,904, released to you at €992 a month after you arrive. France requires a Campus France interview through the Études en France portal. Italy needs Universitaly pre-enrolment and a declaration of value on your certificates. Hungary runs its own Stipendium Hungaricum cycle with a Ministry of Education nomination. Poland, Finland, Sweden and the Netherlands are the most straightforward. We start whichever step applies to you first, because these run alongside your university application, not after it.
How long does a European student visa take from Bangladesh?
Plan for two to three months from lodgement, though it varies by embassy and season. The bottleneck from Bangladesh is usually not the decision but the steps before it: certificate attestation through the Ministry of Education and MOFA, embassy legalisation, and — for Germany — the APS certificate and blocked account. Those alone can take two to three months, so begin the paperwork well before your offer arrives.
Can I stay and work after graduating?
Yes, in all eight. Finland grants twenty-four months, Germany eighteen, France, Italy, Sweden, Poland and the Netherlands twelve, and Hungary nine. You apply from inside the country before your student permit runs out, and the permit carries full work rights with no employer sponsorship needed. One warning: the permit belongs to the country you studied in. You cannot study in Poland and then use Germany's job-seeker visa, so decide where you want to work before you decide where to apply.
Can I settle permanently in Europe after studying?
Yes, and the usual route is the same across the EU: student permit, then the job-search permit, then an EU Blue Card once you have a graduate-level job paying roughly €45,300 or more. Blue Card time counts towards long-term residence, and the card transfers between member states — so the country you study in need not be the country you settle in. The practical requirement almost everywhere is local language at around B1 level, which is worth starting in your first term rather than your last.
Are there scholarships available for Bangladeshi students in Europe?
Yes, and Europe is unusually generous here. Stipendium Hungaricum is the standout: Bangladesh is an official sending partner through the Ministry of Education, with 200–250 fully funded places a year covering tuition, accommodation and a monthly stipend. Erasmus Mundus joint master's degrees cover tuition, travel and a stipend across multiple countries. Finnish universities routinely waive 50–100% of tuition for strong applicants. Italy's regional DSU awards can cover accommodation and meals on top of tuition. Germany has DAAD, and France has the Eiffel Excellence programme. We apply to several in parallel rather than betting the plan on one.
Should I choose Europe over Canada, UK or Australia?
Europe's strongest card is cost. A German public university charges no tuition at all, and the total for a year in Poland or Italy can be under a third of what the same year costs in Australia or the USA. You also apply to several countries in one cycle, which spreads your visa risk. Against that, post-study work is shorter than Canada's, most countries expect local language for long-term work, and the paperwork from Bangladesh — attestation, legalisation, blocked accounts — is heavier than for the English-speaking destinations. Choose Europe if cost is the deciding factor, Canada for the clearest residency route, the UK for speed. We will give you an honest comparison for your profile.
